# Domain Coverage Checklist - israeli-market-fit

Generated: 2026-06-02, refreshed 2026-11-23, via research on: cbs.gov.il (population release), Israel Democracy Institute Haredi Yearbook 2025, taxsummaries.pwc.com (VAT), sii.org.il (standards), nevo.co.il + gov.il (consumer and privacy law), Globes (payment apps), Wikipedia (Yad2), Kol-Zchut (average wage).

## Must cover (core)

- [ ] Israeli market segmentation - split the addressable market into real sub-markets, not "Israel = one market": secular/traditional Hebrew mainstream, Haredi/ultra-Orthodox (14.3% of the population, distinct media, channels, kashrut/modesty gates), Arab-Israeli (21.1%, Arabic-first, separate retail/social graph), new olim (Hebrew-light, diaspora habits), Russian-speaking, periphery/development towns vs Gush Dan center. - source: https://www.idi.org.il/haredi/2025/?chapter=62168 - why core: a product that fits Tel Aviv often dies in Bnei Brak or Nazareth.
- [ ] Market sizing method (TAM/SAM/SOM, bottom-up) - convert segment population times adoption times spend into a credible serviceable number; flag that Israel's whole market is about 10.305 million people, so global SaaS economics rarely translate. - source: https://www.cbs.gov.il/he/mediarelease/DocLib/2026/282/11_26_282b.pdf - why core: the verdict hinges on whether the SOM clears the cost floor.
- [ ] Payment and purchasing culture - P2P/app payment is dominated by Bit (Bank Hapoalim) and paybox (Discount Bank); cash still matters in Haredi/Arab segments; credit-card installments (תשלומים) are an expectation, not a perk. - source: https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-payment-apps-bit-and-paybox-to-charge-fees-from-january-1001483104 - why core: a checkout that omits installments loses conversions; Bit can be added through a gateway as an extra option.
- [ ] Distribution / channel reality - where Israelis actually transact: Yad2 (a major classifieds site), WhatsApp commerce, Facebook groups, local marketplaces vs thin domestic app-store volume. - source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yad2 - why core: "build it and they will come" fails; channel fit is part of market fit.
- [ ] Regulatory gates to bring a product to market - an official standard (תקן רשמי) declared by the Minister of Economy and Industry can block import or sale of regulated goods until the required conformity route is met. Food and cosmetics run through Ministry of Health registration tracks. - source: https://www.sii.org.il/he/israelistandards/ - why core: a physical product can be commercially dead on arrival without an official-standard path.
- [ ] Hebrew labeling requirement - consumer-product packaging generally must carry clear Hebrew labeling even when imported. - source: https://weksler-davidovich.co.il/consumer-protection-labeling/ - why core: a common cost and operational gate for physical consumer goods.
- [ ] Consumer Protection Law - bans deceptive marketing and mandates final-price display. - source: https://www.nevo.co.il/law_html/law00/70305.htm - why core: defines the legal envelope for marketing claims and pricing UI.
- [ ] Direct-marketing / consent regime - the anti-spam law (Communications Law s.30א) requires prior explicit consent for promotional email, SMS and other electronic messages, subject to the narrow s.30א(ג) existing-customer exception and the single consent-request to a business in s.30א(ב). Live marketing calls are separately limited by the Do-Not-Call register (Consumer Protection Law s.16ב and s.16ג). Amendment 13 (in force 14 August 2025) separately governs the customer database. - source: Communications Law s.30א (Wikisource entry in evidence.json) and https://www.nevo.co.il/law_html/law00/70305.htm (s.16ב, s.16ג) - why core: the email/SMS go-to-market channel is legally gated upfront.
- [ ] Pricing localization - one total shekel price including any VAT that applies, benchmarked against the statutory average wage under s.1 of the National Insurance Law (13,566 NIS/month as of January 2026, which is not the CBS economy-wide average), installment-friendly. Consumer prices must be the total price and in Israeli currency only (Consumer Protection Law s.17א, s.17ד), with the narrow s.17ז exemptions the only route to a USD consumer price. s.1 defines a consumer by mainly personal, household or family use; whether a given plan's sales are consumer sales, including self-serve signups, is a question for an advocate. - source: https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/israel/corporate/other-taxes - why core: a price that ignores VAT and local wages reads as overpriced, and a non-total or USD consumer price is unlawful.
- [ ] Seasonality - demand swings around the Jewish-holiday calendar (Rosh Hashana, Pesach, Sukkot), Ramadan for the Arab segment, summer/August, and reserve-duty (miluim) disruption. - source: https://www.cbs.gov.il/he/mediarelease/DocLib/2026/282/11_26_282b.pdf - why core: launch and ad-spend timing depend on the local calendar.
- [ ] Buyer psychology and trust signals - Israeli buyers are price-aggressive, recommendation-driven (WhatsApp word-of-mouth), skeptical of hype; Hebrew UI and local support raise trust. - source: https://www.nevo.co.il/law_html/law00/70305.htm - why core: positioning angles must match how Israelis decide.
- [ ] Competitor / substitute scan - map local incumbents and the "good enough free WhatsApp/Excel/Yad2" substitute before claiming a gap. - source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yad2 - why core: most "no competitor in Israel" claims are wrong once local substitutes are listed.
- [ ] Buyer-avatar construction - segment-specific avatar cards (language, income band, channels, objections, trust triggers, payment method), not one generic persona. - source: https://www.idi.org.il/haredi/2025/?chapter=62168 - why core: this is the skill's core output, grounded in real segment data (Haredi median age 16, 57% under 20).
- [ ] Viability verdict framework (go / pivot / no-go) - an explicit commercial decision rule combining SOM size, channel fit, competitive density, and the budgeted time and cost of resolving flagged regulatory questions with an advocate. - source: https://www.cbs.gov.il/he/mediarelease/DocLib/2026/282/11_26_282b.pdf - why core: this is the deliverable the user actually wants.
- [ ] Global-vs-Israel delta - contrast what works in the global/US market against Israeli realities (much smaller market, RTL/Hebrew, VAT-inclusive pricing, holiday calendar, regulatory gates) and name what must change. - source: https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/israel/corporate/other-taxes - why core: the skill exists to translate a global thesis into an Israel-specific verdict.

## Should cover (advanced / edge cases)

- [ ] RTL/Hebrew product and UX readiness - Hebrew plus Arabic localization, RTL layout, Hebrew SEO as a fit precondition.
- [ ] Diaspora / dual-market plays - olim and Russian/English-speaking sub-markets that bridge Israel and abroad.
- [ ] Kashrut / modesty / religious-fit gates - for food, fashion, media, leisure aimed at observant segments.
- [ ] Import duty / customs path - duties and purchase tax on top of VAT for imported goods affect landed cost.
- [ ] B2B / SaaS / government GTM - size by accounts not population; tiny domestic logo count; Israel often a design-partner/beachhead not a revenue market; tenders (מכרז), resellers, integrators; NIS vs USD contracting.
- [ ] Parallel / grey imports and the single-importer model - for physical brands the competitor is often a grey-market parallel importer or the entrenched official importer.
- [ ] Retail-chain gatekeepers - getting on a shelf can run through an importer or distributor and through chains such as Shufersal.
- [ ] Personal-import (de minimis) substitute - buyers ordering directly from foreign sites under the personal-import tax exemption are a real competitor to local sellers. Currently 75 USD per shipment, with shipping and insurance excluded only when priced separately, and parcels from one supplier to one customer within 72 hours counted as one shipment; orders set it at 150 and then 130 before a Knesset vote returned it to 75 on 01.06.2026, so it must be re-checked each cycle rather than treated as a constant.
- [ ] Election-season conditions - a Knesset campaign changes ad inventory, attention, and platform rules (TikTok bans paid political advertising in Israel, bans paying creators to promote a party or candidate, bans boosting political video, and requires labeling of realistic AI content). The 26th Knesset was elected on 27.10.2026; re-check coalition and budget status each cycle. Election law added a disclosure duty on publishers of deep-fake election propaganda, and rules published in Reshumot on 26.7.2026 set how to comply.
- [ ] Amendment 13 is actively enforced - the Privacy Protection Authority issued its first administrative fine under the amendment in July 2026 for late breach notification, so a marketing database carries live regulatory exposure rather than theoretical duties.
- [ ] Payment-rail mechanics - cards via local gateways/acquirers; confirm that any gateway supports Israeli installments; Bit/paybox are P2P, not the business checkout.
- [ ] Mean-vs-median wage caveat - an average wage is a weak affordability measure; benchmark to local substitute prices, not the mean.
- [ ] National-religious (dati-leumi) segment - mainstream-reachable but with kashrut/values filters, distinct from secular and Haredi.
- [ ] Reserve-duty / wartime demand shocks - miluim call-ups and security events distort B2C demand.
- [ ] Periphery logistics and delivery economics - last-mile cost and coverage gaps outside the center.
- [ ] Distance-sale cancellation detail - later-of-document clock (s.14ג(ג)), four months for disabled, 65+ and new-oleh consumers after a sales conversation (s.14ג1(ג)), the s.14ג(ד) exclusions, and the 5% or 100 NIS fee cap, with no fee at all for a defect or breach (s.14ה).
- [ ] Subscription renewal - fixed-term auto-renewal clause void subject to exceptions (s.13א), any-time cancellation only for Fourth Schedule fixed-term types (s.13א1), three business days to end (s.13ד(ג)), dedicated home-page cancellation link (s.14ט(ב)).
