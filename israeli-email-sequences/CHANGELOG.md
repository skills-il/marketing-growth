# Changelog

## 1.3.0 - 2026-08-24

Rebuilt the Chok HaSpam section around the actual text of section 30A. Added the mandatory "פרסומת" subject-line tag (30A(e)(1)(a)), which the skill had never stated in its body, the full name/address/contact disclosure list, the SMS carve-out in 30A(e)(2) that reduces that list to name plus refusal contact, the three-limb existing-customer exception in 30A(c), and the non-profit route in 30A(b1). Removed the "2 business days" opt-out deadline: the statute sets no period, and the 48-hour rule is Google's and Yahoo's, not Israeli law.

Corrected three Amendment 13 errors that survived the 1.2.1 fix in the English Gotchas and the reference guide: serious security incidents are reported immediately under regulation 11(d) of the 2017 Data Security Regulations, not on a 24-hour clock; there is no 3.2M NIS figure; and there is no flat 5%-of-turnover fine cap, since section 23כו sets fixed amounts and the 5% is a reduction the violator must apply for. Added the section 17ו direct-mail duty to print the database registration number, and corrected the repealed 17ג citation. Removed the DPO headcount confusion by listing the four section 17ב1 triggers.

Removed two fabricated claims: gmail.co.il is a Google-owned parked domain held on MarkMonitor, not Walla Mail, and no post-Amendment-13 EU adequacy review has concluded (the last concluded review is COM(2024) 7 final of 15 January 2024). Dropped the unsupported ActiveTrail/Bird acquisition hedge.

Refreshed deliverability: added Microsoft's 5,000/day requirement effective 5 May 2025, Google's permanent bulk-sender status and November 2025 enforcement ramp, the two distinct spam-rate figures (0.10% guideline, 0.30% hard line) in place of a single 0.3%, the 48-hour unsubscribe deadline, and the marketing-only scope of one-click unsubscribe. Corrected "DKIM2 rollout in progress": DKIM2 is an IETF Internet-Draft with no RFC.

Added four things an expert review found missing for someone actually building a sequence: that the burden of proving consent is the advertiser's and what a defensible consent record holds; double opt-in mechanics, including that the confirmation email is not a דבר פרסומת and must not be tagged; enforcement of Shabbat and memorial-day blackouts inside TRIGGERED flows rather than only broadcasts, which the description had been promising without delivering; and the list-hygiene half of deliverability (warmup, bounce handling, a suppression list that survives ESP migration and CSV re-import, engagement segmentation, sunset). Added the channel-scope reading: the statute defines an electronic message as one carried over the internet and a short message as one carried over a telecom network to terminal equipment, so WhatsApp, Telegram and push sit in the full-disclosure limb rather than the SMS carve-out. Flagged which post-purchase steps cross the advertising boundary, and noted that each duty is satisfied in a different layer (subject field, footer block, ESP header setting).

Replaced the unsourced 22-28% open-rate benchmark with ActiveTrail's 2026 Israeli report (~40% open, ~3% click) plus the Apple Mail Privacy Protection caveat. Corrected the bundled-script filename, which pointed at a file that does not exist, and taught the script to check the subject-line tag. Fixed Rav Messer's domain (responder.co.il), softened the unverifiable "Israeli ESPs ship RTL templates and holiday calendars" claim, replaced the 404 Resend RTL link, added a Recommended MCP Servers section pointing at Hebcal, and reconciled the cart-abandonment timing between the sequence table and Example 2. Fixed the validator's List-Unsubscribe check, which grepped template HTML for a message header and so warned on every correctly built email. Wrote Hebrew statute letters (17ב1, 17ד-17ו, 23כו) instead of unciteable Latin transliterations. Added a legal notice, since the skill now states statutory duties and exposure.

## 1.2.1 - 2026-08-11

Corrected an Amendment 13 threshold conflation. 100,000 is the notification threshold for specially-sensitive databases, not a DPO-appointment trigger. Added the 10,000-person registration duty that applies to direct-mail databases, which is the threshold this skill's users are most likely to cross. Removed the 24-hour breach-reporting deadline and the 3.2M NIS fine figure; neither appears on the Privacy Protection Authority's Amendment 13 guidance.
